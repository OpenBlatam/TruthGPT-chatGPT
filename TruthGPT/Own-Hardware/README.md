# 💻 Creating Your Own Hardware

## ⚙️ Introduction

Avoid misinformation and incomplete distros by building your **own hardware** with **centralized**, **decentralized**, and **hybrid** approaches. The goal is to craft hardware systems using high-quality components such as **Xilinx** for **FPGA** or **ASIC** designs, tailored to your needs.

We outline a reliable and methodical approach to designing your own hardware from scratch.

## 🛠️ How to Create Your Own Chip

### 1. **Create the Framework Code for Your Project**
   - Start with a solid framework to outline the project. This will act as a blueprint for your hardware design.

### 2. **Create Without Frameworks Approach**
   - After planning with frameworks, try the approach of building directly without frameworks to gain a deeper understanding of the hardware logic.

### 3. **Develop Your Own Language (if necessary)**
   - Consider creating a custom hardware description language (HDL) if it offers benefits to the specificity of your hardware needs.

### 4. **Work with Verilog on a Server with FPGA or Pre-fabricated ASIC**
   - Use **Verilog** to describe your chip architecture and test it on a **server with FPGA** or use pre-fabricated **ASIC** chips for prototyping.

### 5. **Construct Your Own Server Using CNC Methods or Providers**
   - You can create your own server using **CNC (Computer Numerical Control)** methods or align with providers that match your hardware and software design rationale.

### 6. **Deploy and "Open" the Framework Design**
   - After constructing and testing, make your framework design **open-source** to allow others to benefit and contribute to the process.

## 🏗️ Building Your Chip with the Right Tools

- Avoid **Bazel** and instead create your **own Verilog build system** tailored to your specific tech design needs. This custom build system can help in streamlining the process of chip design, debugging, and deploying to hardware platforms.

## 🔌 Providers of Dedicated Servers

You’ll need reliable servers for your chip design process. Some providers offer servers and cloud solutions capable of supporting FPGA and ASIC design flows, including high-performance computing needs.
